Two Hand Tide Clock — Gullane Leith-based

Why the second hand?

Most tide clocks have one hand that gives an indication of when there are high and low tides.

However, tides depend on the positions of both the Moon and the Sun. When the Sun and Moon hands align, their effects add together and we get spring tides, with the highest highs and lowest lows. When the Sun and Moon hands point in opposite directions, they partly cancel and we get neap tides. Between neap and spring tides, the Sun delays high and low tides by up to about 40 minutes, known as lagging. Between spring and neap tides, the Sun brings high and low tides forward by up to about 40 minutes, known as priming.

With our tide clock, you can keep it simple and fit one hand. However, if you choose to fit two hands, it will give you a much more nuanced understanding of the tides.

Air pressure

Live pressure comes from Open-Meteo every 30 minutes. 1 cm of tide per hPa from 1013 hPa.

Caution

Predictions are approximate; check an official tide table before swimming or boating.

Both places use the Admiralty Fidra secondary-port differences on Leith. Wind surge is not included.

Check against the official tables

Compared with the NTSLF Leith predictions for 22 Oct – 18 Nov 2026 (108 high and low waters), the online clock averages 7.9 min and 5 cm of difference (worst 33 min, 0.19 m). The mean timing bias is −1.5 min, so there is no time-zone or phase error.

Weather (not included above)

Air pressure moves the sea about 1 cm per hPa, roughly ±0.3 m from 980 to 1040 hPa, and the live pressure feed corrects for this. Wind surge isn't modelled. Whatever difference is left on the day is weather.

How it works

14.77 dSprings to springs
4.8 mSpring range, 2×(1.80+0.61)
2.4 mNeap range, 2×(1.80−0.61)

Two centred hands: the Moon hand is M2, with Leith's real amplitude of 1.80 m, turning once every 12 h 25 m 14 s; the Sun hand is S2, amplitude 0.61 m, turning once every 12 h. The Sun hand slowly gains, so the hands line up every 14.77 days (springs) and point opposite ways in between (neaps).

Each hand's height is its amplitude times the cosine of its angle from straight up. Add the two to the 3.29 m mean level and you read the tide; the height sum table does the adding, plus the smaller terms.

The physical prototype

Moon hand (M2): a commercial "time and tide" quartz movement, one turn per 12 h 25 m.

Sun hand (S2): an ordinary 12 h hand.

Assembly: both hands centred on the same pivot, lengths in the ratio of their Leith amplitudes, 1.80 m to 0.61 m (about 3:1).
